How the AI Thinks: Algorithm & Mathematical Proof

Combinatorial Game Theory, Misère Nim & P-Positions

▾

1. Misère Terminal Condition

Under misère play, taking the final stick loses. Therefore, having 1 stick left on your turn is an unavoidable loss (a P-position / Previous-player-winning position).

2. Losing Positions ($n \pmod 5 = 1$)

From $n \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$, the moving player can choose $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ sticks respectively to leave exactly $1$ stick for the opponent. Thus, $\{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ are winning positions (N-positions).

From $n = 6$, any move of $1..4$ leaves $5, 4, 3,$ or $2$ sticks, enabling the opponent to force $1$. Hence, $6$ is a losing position. By induction, the losing positions up to 21 are:

3. The $5 - x$ Invariant

Whenever the human takes $x$ sticks from a losing pile, the AI immediately counters with $5 - x$. Because $x + (5 - x) = 5$, the pile monotonically drops:

$$21 \xrightarrow{-5} 16 \xrightarrow{-5} 11 \xrightarrow{-5} 6 \xrightarrow{-5} 1$$

The human is mathematically trapped and must take the terminal stick.
